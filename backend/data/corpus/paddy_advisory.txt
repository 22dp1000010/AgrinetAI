Water management: Maintain 2-5 cm standing water during tillering. If NDVI or field
observation shows declining vigour with low rainfall forecast, irrigate immediately;
moisture stress at panicle initiation can cut yield by 20-30 percent. Practise
alternate wetting and drying (AWD) where water is scarce: irrigate when water level
falls 15 cm below soil surface in a field water tube.

Brown planthopper (BPH, Nilaparvata lugens): High humidity (above 75 percent) with
temperatures of 28-32 C favours outbreaks. Watch the base of hills for hopper burn
(circular patches of drying plants). Drain water from the field for 3-4 days to
reduce hopper population. Avoid excess nitrogen; skip a urea top dressing if hopper
presence is confirmed. Open alleyways of 30 cm after every 2-3 metres of planting.
Do not apply combination insecticide mixtures without KVK confirmation. If more than
10 hoppers per hill are seen, consult the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra before any
chemical spray.

Rice blast (Magnaporthe oryzae): Intermittent rain with cloudy weather favours leaf
and neck blast. Spindle-shaped lesions with grey centres on leaves are diagnostic.
Avoid excess nitrogen. Use silicon sources (rice husk ash) to strengthen tissue.
Seed treatment and need-based fungicide only per label and KVK guidance.

Nutrient management: Apply nitrogen in three splits (basal, tillering, panicle
initiation). In soils with pH below 6.0, monitor zinc deficiency (khaira disease);
apply zinc sulphate 25 kg per hectare if leaves show bronzing.
