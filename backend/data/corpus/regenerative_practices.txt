Soil organic carbon: Indian soils average below 5 g per kg soil organic carbon;
regenerative practice aims to raise it. Retain crop residues, apply compost or
farmyard manure, and keep living roots in the soil as long as possible.

Green manuring: Grow dhaincha (Sesbania) or sunhemp before paddy and incorporate
at flowering (40-50 days). Adds 60-80 kg nitrogen per hectare and improves soil
structure in one season.

Cover crops and intercropping: Legume intercrops (green gram, black gram, cowpea)
fix nitrogen, suppress weeds and reduce pest pressure. In widely spaced crops
(cotton, chilli, maize) intercropping is the fastest regenerative entry point.

Reduced tillage: Zero-till drilling of rabi crops into standing kharif residue
saves one irrigation, reduces cost, and prevents residue burning.

Water stewardship: Alternate wetting and drying in paddy cuts water use by about
25 percent and methane emissions by 30-50 percent without yield loss when managed
with a field water tube.

Agroforestry and bunds: Plant Gliricidia or subabul on field bunds for green leaf
manure. Vetiver lines on sloping fields cut soil erosion sharply.
